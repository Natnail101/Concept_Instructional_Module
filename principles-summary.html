How I Used the Multimedia and Contiguity Principles

Back to Module

This programmed tutorial teaches a use-level concept: deciding whether an image is accessible by comparing the actual image to the image’s alt text and deciding whether the alt text communicates purpose. I revised the project so learners no longer judge alt text by itself. They now see the image, the page scenario, the alt text, worked examples, practice questions, diagnostic feedback, and a final assessment.

Multimedia principle: I used words and visuals together. Each worked example and practice item includes a visual image, a captioned scenario, and the alt text being evaluated. This helps learners understand that alt text is not only about naming an object. It is about explaining the purpose of the image for someone who cannot see it.

Contiguity principle: I kept the related information close together. The image, scenario, alt text, answer choices, and feedback appear on the same screen or in the directly connected “Show me why” explanation. This reduces split attention because learners do not have to search across pages to compare the image and its alt text.

I also used signaling by emphasizing the words purpose, action, context, weak alt text, and better alt text. The diagnostic remediation screens break the problem into tutor-like steps: what the image shows, what the image is for, what the alt text gives, and what a stronger alt text version would include.
